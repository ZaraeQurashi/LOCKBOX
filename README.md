# LOCKBOX
Lockbox is a minimalist, 3D-printed puzzle vault that conceals its contents behind hidden mechanical locks. It combines minimalist geometric design with interactive puzzle while challenging users to discover the correct sequence to unlock it.

 ## Overview

LOCKBOX is a CAD and 3D-printing project focused on mechanical design, problem-solving, and creating a functional puzzle box. The goal was to design a storage container that conceals its locking mechanism behind a clean, minimalist exterior, making it difficult to tell how the box opens.

My original design involved more complicated locking mechanisms, but I encountered significant scaling and assembly issues when combining my separate STL files in Fusion 360. Rather than continue with a design that was not working, I decided to start from scratch and redesign the entire box around simpler sliding mechanisms.

The final design consists of a main body, external side panels, internal sliding panels, an outer casing, a dovetail lid, and a locking bar. Each component serves a specific purpose in the assembly and unlocking process.

## Features

- Concealed locking mechanism: The outer casing hides the internal sliding components.

- Sliding panels: Internal rails and panels create the puzzle mechanism.

- Modular construction: Separate components make the design easier to model and assemble.

- Dovetail joint lid: A shaped connector joins the lid to the outer casing.

- Locking bar: An additional component restricts disassembly until the correct sequence is followed.

- Minimalist exterior: The simple outer shape hides the complexity of the internal design.

- Designed for 3D printing: Components are modeled in Fusion 360 for eventual physical fabrication.

# Design Process

1. Main Body and Base

The main body established the dimensions and internal storage space for the box. Instead of modeling the entire enclosure as one solid object, I separated it into panels so I could adjust and inspect each component individually. I used sketches to define the geometry and extruded them into 3D shapes. The base became the reference for the rest of the design, helping determine the alignment of the other components.

2. External Side Panels

The external side panels form the outer walls of the main enclosure. I designed them with an upside-down L-shaped profile and an overhang to help the pieces fit around the body. After sketching and extruding the geometry, I added screw-head-like details and used mirroring to create symmetrical components. This reduced repetitive modeling while keeping both sides consistent.

![image](https://halflife.hackclub-assets.com/hackclub-half-life/sessions/EgY3s8kanBhx0FNjeHxRsejCAPHT6ftU/61674b2c385dd070c33c243d80b5c41dda9b8953e040dcfd0b476253180e78b6.png)

3. Internal Sliding Panels

The internal panels form the core of the puzzle mechanism. I wanted the box to require some thought to open without making the mechanism too complicated to design and manufacture.
I created E-shaped panels with upper and lower rails that guide the sliding components. I then mirrored the design to produce the matching sides. The sliding arrangement provides a physical obstacle while keeping the mechanism relatively simple.

4. Outer Casing and Dovetail Lid

The outer casing gives LOCKBOX its minimalist appearance and conceals the internal sliding mechanism. I designed it to fit around the internal assembly while hiding the components that would otherwise reveal how the box opens. For the lid, I applied what I had learned about dovetail joints in CAD. The lid uses a simple rectangular top with a trapezoidal connector that fits into the outer casing, creating a cleaner appearance without adding unnecessary external details.

5. Locking Bar

The locking bar is an additional component that helps hold the assembly together. I modeled it as a bracket-shaped piece with a notch that interacts with the main assembly. The bar and outer body have corresponding notches, meaning the components must be positioned in the correct sequence during assembly and disassembly. This adds another layer to the puzzle without requiring a complicated combination lock.

## Challenges and Problem-Solving

My biggest challenge was discovering that my original components would not fit together as intended. I had created several separate STL files including a working rotational lock, but differences in scale, alignment, and component geometry made assembly difficult. Since the deadline was approaching, continuing to repair the original design was no longer practical.I researched alternative puzzle-box mechanisms and chose to rebuild LOCKBOX around sliding components. This allowed me to develop a more manageable design while preserving the original goal of creating a concealed locking mechanism.

The redesign also changed how I approached CAD. Instead of building complicated components independently and trying to combine them at the end, I used the main body as a reference and designed the remaining pieces around it. This helped me focus more on the relationships between components and how they would fit together.

## What I Learned

This project taught me the importance of accurate measurements and accounting for clearances when designing objects with multiple moving parts. I also gained practical experience using Fusion 360's sketching, extrusion, mirroring, and joint-design techniques.

Most importantly, I learned that successful engineering involves adapting to setbacks. By simplifying the mechanism and changing my workflow, I was able to complete the CAD design within my deadline while retaining the key features of the original concept.

# Final Result

LOCKBOX represents my progression from an ambitious but difficult initial concept to a more practical mechanical design. The completed CAD model brings together a concealed sliding mechanism, modular panels, a dovetail lid, and a locking bar in a minimalist enclosure.
